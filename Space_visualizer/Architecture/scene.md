# scene (`src/scene`)

Back to [[Home]] · [[Overview]] · [[render]]

- **FrameState:** a mutable per-frame cache (positions, validity, orientations, camera position), shared through context and never stored in React state.
- **Scale modes:** `renderRadiusKm` keeps the real radius in true-scale mode. In readable mode it enforces a minimum on-screen size (10 px for the Sun, 5 px for other bodies).
- **Bodies:** ellipsoids with PCK radii, oriented by the IAU rotation models (sphere geometry rotated so its poles lie on +Z). Surfaces use official NASA/USGS/STScI imagery where a body has a map; procedural layers (Venus clouds, Sun surface, ring tint, band flow, cloud drift, exaggerated relief) are labelled illustrative.
- **Orbit lines:** rebuilt every frame relative to the camera, so they stay precise next to the planet.
- **Light:** there are no three.js lights. Each body shader lights itself from `uSunDirObj` (Sun direction in the mesh's object space, computed in float64 on the CPU); bloom and the rest of the post-processing stack are described in [[render]].
- **Labels:** drei `<Html>` rendered into a fixed `.label-layer` portal (`LabelLayerContext`). `BodyMesh` positions at priority −0.5 so labels never project from the camera's own position (which would cache `NaN`).
- **SceneRoot:** `div.scene` (an isolated stacking context, so the panels stay above the labels) containing the Canvas and the label layer.

## Rendering (M2a.1)

See also the M2 spec [[2026-10-04-m2-cinematic-and-rich-bodies-design]] and decision [[0005-webgl2-custom-shaders]].

- **Object-space shading:** every body shader works in the mesh's object space, where the unit sphere is the rendered equatorial radius and the surface point is `vec3(vUnit.xy, vUnit.z * uFlattening)`. The Sun and camera directions (`uSunDirObj`, `uCamObj`) are computed on the CPU in float64 by `bodyUniforms.ts`; `useBodyUniforms` writes them every frame at `useFrame` priority −0.4.
- **Materials:** `createBodyMaterial` prepends the shared GLSL chunks (`chunks.ts`: noise, lighting, eclipse) to each fragment. The material follows `render.shader`: `EarthMaterial`, `RockyMaterial`, `GiantMaterial` (illustrative band flow), `VenusMaterial` (illustrative cloud deck) and `SunMaterial` (illustrative granulation and limb darkening).
- **BodyExtras:** shells drawn with a body: `CloudShell` (Earth clouds, drift illustrative, cloud shadows), `AtmosphereShell` (O'Neil scattering: GroundFromSpace for rays that hit the planet, SkyFromSpace with a fit-error clamp for sky rays), `RingShell` (Saturn and Uranus rings from the NSSDCA ring tables, planet shadow on the rings and ring shadow on the globe, lit and unlit faces; the file is `RingShell.tsx`, not `Rings.tsx`, which would collide with `rings.ts` on Windows) and `CoronaGlow` (Sun).
- **Log depth:** every `ShaderMaterial` includes three's `logdepthbuf` chunks. Surface shaders end with the tonemapping and colour-space chunks.
- **Sky:** a sphere that follows the camera, textured with the NASA Deep Star Maps (2k and 4k committed, credit in the app).
- **Tiered loading:** `useTieredTexture` loads the best tier up to `viewStore.textureTier` (default 2k), falls back to lower tiers, disposes superseded textures and cancels stale loads. `TextureReadiness` sets `data-textures-ready` on the scene so e2e screenshots wait for the textures.
- **URL overrides:** `jd`, `date`, `pause`, `target`, `dist`, `az`, `azSun`, `el` and `tier` (see `urlParams.ts`) set a reproducible view; the golden screenshots use them.
- **Disposal:** every material and geometry created in `useMemo` is disposed on change and unmount.

## The Moon (M2b.2)
- **Positions:** the frame driver uses `systemState`, so Earth and the Moon move about their barycentre. The orbit line is `SatelliteOrbitLine.tsx`: the geocentric path of one lunar month, sampled with the one model in force at the date and repositioned around Earth's current position every frame (it is not parented to Earth), disposed on change.
- **Hiding rule (`satelliteVisibility.ts`, `BodyMesh.tsx`):** a satellite is hidden only when it is inside its parent's drawn disc plus its own radius plus a 4 px margin AND readable-scale inflation causes it (the satellite or the parent is drawn larger than its true equatorial radius, tolerance 1e-6). With true radii on both, it is never hidden, so a close Moon seen past Earth stays visible. The rule is binary (no fade) and applies in both scale modes (`satelliteHidden`, `isInflated`). A hidden body adds its id to `frame.bodyHidden`, its raycast is off and its label is hidden. Selecting a hidden Moon still flies the camera to it, and it shows as the camera closes in.
- **Minimum size:** `MIN_PIXELS` is `{ star: 10, body: 5, moon: 3 }` (`scaleMode.ts`).
- **Tour:** the Moon is a stop, ordered by `meanDistanceFromParentKm`.
- **Shading (`RockyMaterial`):** Lommel-Seeliger uses the relief normal for both mu0 and mu, so a full Moon is evenly bright and the terminator keeps its relief. **Earthshine** lights the night side from Earth's direction with strength `EARTHSHINE.max * (1 + cos phi) / 2`, where phi is the angle at Earth between the Sun and the Moon. It applies only to Earth's moons (`receivesEarthshine`).
- **Eclipse tint:** `occluderVisibility` is shared by `eclipseFactor` and the new `eclipseLight`. When the occluder has an atmosphere (a data flag, `render.atmosphere !== null`), the remaining light is mixed toward `ECLIPSE_TINT.color` (copper red), and the umbra keeps a floor of light. `pickOccluders` puts Earth first for the Moon and the Moon first for Earth.
- **Atmosphere eclipse shade:** `AtmosphereShell` multiplies its in-scatter by `eclipseFactor` at the path midpoint, so the Moon's shadow shows through Earth's haze (golden 6d). It applies to every shell.
- **Illustrative constants** (`illustrative.ts`, labelled in the drawer): `EARTHSHINE` { max 0.02 } and `ECLIPSE_TINT` { color (1, 0.30, 0.08), amount 0.9, floor 0.12, centreDarkening 0.5 }. Reasons are in [[2026-10-29-m2b2-moon]].
- **Goldens:** six views (6 to 6f) in `e2e/golden.spec.ts`; screenshots in `Assets/m2b2/`.

## Explore interior (M2b.3)
Built in [[2026-10-30-m2b3-explore-interior]]. Files are in `src/scene/interior/`.
- **Technique.** Each layer is a group at its explode offset, turning with the body (orientation frozen at entry, written into `frame.bodyOrientation`). It holds three parts: a **cut face** (a disc on the cut plane, coloured by the ellipsoidal radius of each fragment, so a plane that sweeps in shows rings at the right size), an **outer surface** (a sphere at the layer's outer radius) and a **hollow** (a back-faced sphere at its inner radius). Layer 0's outer surface is the body's own mesh and material.
- **Shader-space cut.** Points with `dot(p, n) > offset` are discarded in the fragment shader, in body-fixed object space. It was chosen over three's clipping planes: the plane is fixed to the body for free, `localClippingEnabled` is not needed and every part shares one uniform. `createBodyMaterial` wraps every body material (`wrapForExplore` renames `main()`): it discards beyond `uClip` (normal xyz, offset w, with a per-shell `clipScale`: clouds 1.006, atmosphere its outer radius, corona its scale) and multiplies the colour by `uDim`.
- **InteriorModel (`InteriorModel.tsx`):** builds the geometry and materials in `useMemo` (disposed on change and unmount; segments follow `PRESETS[level].sphereSegments`) and updates shared uniforms by reference each frame.
- **Views.** *Half-section:* a plane through the centre, its normal across the explode axis toward the camera, then turned 45 degrees to screen-right (`SECTION_YAW_RAD`), so a crescent of the real surface stays in view. The normal is captured when the view opens and on each `sectionSeq` bump, from the camera's orbit state (where it is going). *Exploded:* the plane is horizontal (normal -up). Every layer above the innermost is drawn as an upper cap (dome, base ring, hollow) lifted along the up axis captured at entry; the innermost stays a whole sphere. `cutAmounts` slices the lower halves off over the first 40 % of progress and lifts the caps from 25 % to 100 %. Lower halves ("bowls") were tried on paper and rejected: lifted hollow shells interpenetrate, caps never do.
- **Materials (`interiorMaterial.ts`, `interiorPalette.ts`):** `interiorPalette(state, depth, kind)` gives illustrative ramps (crust grey-brown, rocky mantle rust, liquid metal amber, solid inner core near white, plasma orange to white, hydrogen blue to violet, ices blue to pale). `layerColours` picks the ramp once at the layer's mid-depth and spans 40 % of it, so neighbours step apart as clean rings. Faces and domes are mostly emissive, so the cut reads on the night side. Boundary lines sit on sharp boundaries; their brightness is capped at glow 2.2. A band boundary (a cited range or a cited gradual boundary) has no line and no shading step: the face blends the two layers across the band, a banded dome's limb takes on the colour of the layer above, and the hollow above it the colour of the layer below (illustrative). The Sun's plasma gets `starGain` 2.4 to match the photosphere's HDR level.
- **Saturation push, bounded below zero (Task 8).** Under AgX no non-negative input gets past about `#c89256`, so amber is out of reach. The emission is pushed away from its own grey: `luma + (c - luma) * (1 + saturation * min(glow, 1))`, `INTERIOR_LOOK.saturation` 0.5. The result is **bounded below zero**: `max(pushed, -negativeFloor * luma)`, `negativeFloor` 0.25. AgX mixes about 10 % of red into blue, so amber needs blue a little under zero. A zero clamp brings the pastel look back (salmon, peach). Unbounded reaches about -0.4 on the hottest plasma. The bound keeps the amber (`#d07604`, against `#d17500` unbounded and `#cf7748` clamped) and limits what bloom and blur can smear. SwiftShader shows no artefacts; a real GPU has not been checked (see the session note).
- **Transition driver (`exploreTransition.ts`, `useExploreTransition.ts`):** `stepTransition` is pure. `t` runs at 1/1.2 s (`OPEN_SECONDS`); `focus` (dimming, Sun effects) at 1/0.6 s (`FOCUS_SECONDS`). A view change, a `sectionSeq` bump, a body switch or a leave first closes the current view from wherever it is, then opens the next. Reduced motion jumps. The scene session outlives the store: after `leave()` the store is idle at once, the scene closes the body, and the session ends when `t` and `focus` reach 0. The driver runs at `useFrame` priority -0.9 (after CameraSystem, before BodyMesh), publishes `frame.explore` and mirrors the eased progress into `exploreStore.setProgress` while active. Dev hook: `window.__svExplore()` returns `frame.explore`.
- **`frame.explore`** (type `ExploreFrame`, `exploreFrame.ts`; null outside a session): `bodyId`, `view` (the view being drawn, which lags the store while one closes), `progress`, `focus`, `drawn` (the `DrawnLayer[]`), `offsets` (body radii along `upWorld`), `upWorld` and `upObj` (explode axis, ecliptic and body-fixed), `planeNormalWorld` and `planeNormalObj`, `planeOffset` (1 or more means nothing is cut), `cutsInnermost` (Half-section true, Exploded false), `shiftRadii` (camera look-at shift along up), `halfHeightRadii` (half the drawn extent along up: 1 for the whole body, more while Exploded; the camera fits it) and `selectedLayerId`. Body radii are `frame.bodyRenderRadiusKm.get(bodyId)`. `frame.exploreCamera` (written by CameraSystem) holds `upWorld` and `toCameraWorld`. It is the one source of truth for the labels (ruling R2).
- **Map gaps (`mapGaps.ts`, `GiantMaterial`, Explore only):** while a giant is open, Saturn's black equatorial band (kept black under the rings) is filled from the latitudes either side, and the Hubble OPAL maps of Uranus and Neptune keep to the latitudes they cover (`MAP_COVERAGE`, read from the 1k maps), so no black stripe or stretched streaks cross the opened shell. Illustrative; normal views keep the maps as they are.
- **Ring hiding:** `ringsVisible(bodyId, explore)` is false for the explored body once progress is above 0. The ring mesh and the giant's ring shadow on the globe both go.
- **Dimming:** every other body is dimmed to 25 % (`EXPLORE_DIM`) through the `uDim` uniform in each body material, including clouds, atmosphere, rings and the corona. All pill labels and both kinds of orbit line are hidden during a session. The sky stays.
- **Picking:** an analytic ray test per layer (`rayHitShell`: cut face annulus, clipped outer sphere, hollow) is the `raycast` of each layer's face mesh. A click (under 4 px of drag) calls `selectLayer`. While the body is open its own raycast is off (it would hit the cut-away part). `bodyPickable` also turns off every other body's raycast while `frame.explore` is set, so a click on a dimmed planet in the background does not select it and end Explore. Clicking empty space clears the layer (`clearLayerOnEmptyClick`, the Canvas's `onPointerMissed`). The selected layer is brightened (x1.2) and the others dimmed (x0.35).
- **Labels:** `InteriorLabels.tsx` and `labelAnchors.ts`. See [[ui]].
- **Not covered by unit tests:** the React and WebGL parts, and CameraSystem's Explore path (they need a renderer). The end-to-end tests and the screenshot loop cover them.

## Feature pins on Surface (M2b.4a)
Built in [[2026-11-15-m2b4a-feature-pins]] (Task 9) from spec [[2026-11-15-m2b4a-feature-pins-design]] (§5.1, §6.4). Files: `src/scene/surface/pinAnchors.ts` (pure), `src/scene/surface/FeaturePins.tsx`. The card and list are in [[ui]].
- **Technique:** pins are DOM, like the interior labels, not 3D objects. `FeaturePins` renders into the label layer; `FeaturePinProjector` runs in the Canvas at `useFrame` priority -0.3 and writes transforms, opacity and attributes directly (no React updates per frame). A `PinBridge` object joins the two.
- **Which pins:** `shownPins(details, selectedFeatureId)`: the body's landmarks in rank order, then the selected feature if it is not a landmark (the temporary pin).
- **Position:** `surfacePoint(lat, lonE, flattening)` from [[core]] gives the point on the drawn, flattened shape in the body-fixed frame the texture is mapped in; `pinPoints` turns it by the frozen orientation (`frame.bodyOrientation`) and projects it.
- **Facing (`pinFacing`):** the cosine between the camera direction and the true ellipsoid normal at the point. A dot fades between `PIN_FADE` 0.08 and 0.25 and is hidden below. Pins on the night side keep full brightness (they are interface).
- **Shown dots (`pinDotShown`):** facing, and the centre at least `PIN_DOT_INSET` 11 px inside every edge; a hidden dot leaves the tab order. `.xpin[hidden]` and `.xpin.is-numbered[hidden]` keep the hidden attribute in force over the dots' own `display` rules; `.xdot[hidden]` does the same for the interior phone dots (fixed in Task 11).
- **Names:** `pinNameAnchors` feeds `labelLayout` with the shown pins whose facing is above 0.5, each on its own side of the body's screen centre (`pinSide`), the selected and hovered pins first, then rank. `PIN_NAME_LAYOUT` is `{ maxShiftFraction: 0.07, sameSideOnly: true }`: a name moves at most 7 % of the viewport height and never crosses to the other side (the selected pin is exempt). Reserved rects come from `[data-explore-reserved]`, as for the interior labels. Names and leaders fade over 150 ms; a fading leader stays where it was.
- **When pins show (`pinLayerShown`):** Explore active on this body, store view `whole`, the drawn body closed and the chart shut. Otherwise `hidePins` hides the root and sets every name and leader hidden (a name uses `visibility: inherit`, so it cannot outlive the root). The root's opacity follows `frame.explore.focus`. Interior labels and pins never show together.
- **Clearing:** `clearFeatureOnEmptyClick()` (in `exploreStore.ts`) runs from the Canvas's `onPointerMissed` and from the explored body's click (after the drag check).
- **Placement guards:** `pipeline/textures/placement.test.ts` proves, on the committed maps, that a pin lands on its feature (see [[pipeline]]).
- **Goldens:** six Surface views (`8` to `8f`) in `e2e/golden.spec.ts`; screenshots in `Assets/m2b4a/`. The `7*` Explore views were re-shot for the new entry direction (`Assets/m2b3/`).
